Heart: Detailed Lesson

Anatomy → valves → circulation → coronary supply → cardiac cycle
Let's first orient the heart. In an anterior view, the patient's right side appears on our left. The heart has four chambers and a muscular septum separating the right and left sides.
The superior and inferior venae cavae return deoxygenated blood from the systemic circulation to the right atrium.
During ventricular filling, blood passes from the right atrium through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle.
Right ventricular systole ejects blood through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary trunk and pulmonary arteries, carrying it toward the lungs.
After gas exchange in the lungs, oxygenated blood returns through the pulmonary veins to the left atrium.
Blood then crosses the mitral valve into the left ventricle. The mitral and tricuspid valves are atrioventricular valves.
During left ventricular systole, the aortic valve opens and blood is ejected into the aorta for systemic circulation. The aortic and pulmonary valves are semilunar valves.
The coronary arteries arise from the aortic root and supply the myocardium. The heart therefore has its own arterial blood supply.
Now connect this to the cardiac cycle: ventricular filling occurs mainly during diastole, followed by ventricular contraction and ejection during systole.
Complete pathway: SVC/IVC → RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonary valve → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → LA → mitral → LV → aortic valve → aorta → body.

The problem

DSA is still taught as static pictures.

Students preparing for exams and placement interviews learn recursion, traversals and sorting from slides and recorded videos. A video can't answer “why did it go left here?”, and a chatbot that draws a diagram can draw it wrong. When you are learning an algorithm, a wrong picture is worse than no picture.

The product

A professor you can interrupt, and a tutor for everything else.

Computed lessons

Algorithms, run for real

Pick a lesson or type your own input. Every frame on the board is a state the algorithm actually reached.

  • Stacks and queues, recursion, searching, sorting, binary trees and graphs
  • Your own data, like “binary search for 4 in [9, 3, 7, 1]”
  • The Python line for each step, highlighted as it runs
  • Checkpoints whose answers come from the trace, not a model
AI tutor

Any topic, explained and drawn

Ask about networking, physics or a chapter of your notes. The answer streams in, then a lecture is drawn part by part.

How it works

The AI directs the lesson. It doesn't draw it.

No video model and no guesswork. The engine executes, the AI explains, and a validator checks every scene before you see it.

01

Route

Your topic is matched to an algorithm, and the input values are read from what you typed.

02

Execute

The engine runs the algorithm and records every state: each node visited, each comparison, each swap.

03

Narrate

The trace becomes scenes. The AI may reword the narration, and any line that changes a value is thrown out.

04

Play and branch

Voice, subtitles and the pointer stay in sync. Questions add scenes; a wrong checkpoint branches to a new explanation.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Why can't a computed animation be wrong?

Because nobody draws it. The engine runs the algorithm on your input and records each state. Every frame you see is one of those states, and checkpoint answers are read from the same trace. The AI only writes the spoken explanation, and any line that names a value not in its step is replaced with the computed sentence.

Which topics are computed today?

Fourteen: stacks and queues; recursion (Fibonacci); binary search; bubble, insertion, selection and merge sort; inorder, preorder, postorder and level-order traversal; and breadth-first and depth-first search on graphs. Any other topic goes to the AI tutor, which writes an explanation and draws a lecture with a background fact check. Those lectures are generated, not computed.

How is this different from AI video tools or chatbots?

Those show what a model predicts, and the picture can be wrong. NeuraLearn animates recorded execution states in the browser, and you can interrupt it mid-lesson and get new scenes back. For other topics, the AI tutor's answers are checked in the background and doubtful lines are flagged.

Do I need an account?

No. You can learn as a guest and your progress stays in your browser. Create a free account to keep progress, notes, your streak and saved lectures on every device; whatever you did as a guest comes with you.

Is my data safe?

API keys live only on the server. Your account data is stored with row level security, so only you can read it. PDFs are read in your browser and are never uploaded.

Where does it go after DSA?

Physics with real simulations and maths with a computer algebra system, using the same rule: compute first, explain second.
